# springboot-learning
Hello everyone who might be (un)lucky enough to come across this!

I have been a software developer since graduating in 2021 with my Bachelor's degree in Computer Information Systems (basically Computer Science without all the calculus) and have worked at a point-of-sale solutions development company since graduation. I was hired directly by the CTO at the time, with no technical interview since he was already familiar with my code snippets from browsing my other repos. It was an incredible, once-in-a-lifetime opportunity that I don't think I'll ever get again, and I am proud to call him my mentor and my friend.

During this time, I have become very familiar with Groovy/Grails, Spring Boot, and Angular programming, with a little dabbling in Python and React here and there. I also learned how to set up Ubuntu Linux servers using SaltStack scripts, and help manage our PostgreSQL databases. It is stressful, but still quite fun. The project I have been spearheading and am extremely proud of is a Grails-to-Springboot migration project for one of our top-used order tracking and bookkeeping applications. It's not done yet, but hopefully it will be completed by 2027.

Lately, with the rise of Generative AI and the push for GenAI integration with everything from IDE implementations to agentic automation for development and testing, my company has started increasing the pressure for us grunts to start incorporating these "tools" into our work; ultimately prioritizing speed and end products over readability, maintainability, and quality control. This is the direction development around the world is being driven, and a lot of people believe it cannot be turned around. 

I wholeheartedly disagree with this mindset. 

The bubble is already starting to burst; GenAI models are being developed to prioritize token conservation over spitting out better quality results (probably to drive up their engagement numbers so they look better for investors), and many businesses are seeing that human creation is being sought after more and more because of this decrease in quality - to the point that they are incorporating proof of human creation in their advertisements (look at the Prada Christmas 2026 commercials!).

"Human-made" is on trend to becoming viewed as a luxury, even for things that used to be seen as cheap or just normal day-to-day functions. No one likes AI menus, and no one wants AI-generated ads or articles. While it might be seen as "good" at generating an end product in record time, GenAI is not good at creating something that needs to constantly evolve - like software and games. It cannot generate something that someone else can maintain by themselves; you have to either keep using AI and make the codebase worse, or clean up the mess before it becomes too big to handle. I believe that those of us who do not allow ourselves to fall into the AI trap will not be left behind like so many tech bros claim; rather, I believe we will be the only ones left who can salvage the catastrophic remains once the bubble completely disintegrates.

But right now, I do not have a choice but to use AI in my workflow for my job; the market is too saturated for me to find another job, and most of the jobs on offer (if they aren't ghost jobs just phishing for your info) contain AI-assisted development as a prerequisite. I also just really like my company and don't want to leave; we aren't the "family" type, but they have demonstrated a level of care for their employees that I have not seen or heard of anywhere else. So, what do I do?

That's where this repo (and other ones I create in the future) comes in. I have found through my work with GenAI that I am losing my development skills at an alarming rate, to the point that I almost feel like a freshman again. To stop this, while I do what my work wants and generate code with AI, I'm going to be practicing development on my own with these side projects, starting with the two things I know and have used the most: Spring Boot and Angular.
 
This is the Spring Boot repo, using the latest version available to me as of 2026 - Spring Boot 4.2.0. The ultimate goal is to create a Spring Boot/Angular app, similar to what I am creating for the migration project at work (with different functions of course. You're not getting any company secrets from me!), but right now the repos will be filled with tutorial directories as I go on my "re-skill" journey. I hope you'll stick around and watch me grow.

Welcome to "Getting better at Spring Boot by hand so I don't go insane from the AI slop"
